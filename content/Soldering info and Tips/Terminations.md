
# Solder Terminals

### Different types of terminals

- Bifurcated terminal

- Terminals with top and bottom route connections (most likely bifurcated)

- Slotted terminal

- Cup terminal

### Extra pointers 

- terminals and solder cups shall not be changed to accept oversize conductors. [A1D1D1]

- Flared flange angles should be 35 and 120 degrees. Should extend .3 and 1.5 mm beyond the surface of the land.

- Must have enough insulation clearence. (no solder embeded in connection or change form of connection) [A1D2D3]

- Service Loops, when they are required Do them! if there not, you can still do them! [D1D2D3]

- At least one component lead must have [[Board deformation signs]] [D1D2D3]

- When mounting a leaded device to a terminal, keep a constant direction of wrapping (Clock wise or counter clock wise) for stress relief purposes)
	 Shall continue the dress of the lead/wire



## Terminal Mountings mounts

1. Flat Shoulder
2. Plated through-hole
3. Active circuity
4. Annular ring
5. Flared Flange
6. Board 
### Damage

- Shank must not have circumferential cracks or damage so that flux, oils, inks can get stuck inside. [D1D2D3]

- Terminals shall be mounted with flared flanges in pth's [N1D2D3]

- Terminals shall not be flared to the base material of pcb [N1D2D3]
