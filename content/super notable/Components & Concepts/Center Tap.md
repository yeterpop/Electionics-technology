

There are multiple other uses for center tap, then just using them for transformers:

*"In electronics, a **center tap** (**CT**) is a contact made to a point halfway along a winding of a [transformer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformer "Transformer") or [inductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductor "Inductor"), or along the element of a [resistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistor "Resistor") or a [potentiometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potentiometer "Potentiometer")."*
>*source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_tap*

---

If a transformer has 3 output leads, the center one is the center tap. which is learned about in [[Lab 3-2]] For that lab we used center tap for a Ac to Dc power conversion. 

---

Center tap on a transformer is used for ground and only outputs a single cycle of postive and negative waves. That's what happens when you have static leads. This is different from [[bridge recitfiers]]

*"In a center-tapped rectifier, only half of the secondary winding is used at a time. Thus the transformer size (kVA rating) required for a center-tap rectifier is more than the bridge rectifier."*
>*source: https://electronicsmind.com/difference-between-center-tapped-and-bridge-rectifier/*
